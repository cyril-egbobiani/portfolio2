Most days I switch hats more times than I can count. In the morning I'm in Figma, pushing a table layout around for a compliance dashboard. By the afternoon I'm in the codebase, finding out which of those ideas were realistic.

People sometimes ask which one I really am: a designer who codes or an engineer who designs. Honestly, I've stopped trying to pick. The interesting work happens where the two meet.

## The design file is a promise

A mockup always looks finished. Every name fits on one line, every list has exactly four items, and nothing is ever loading. Then real data shows up.[^1]

Building my own designs has made me a more honest designer. Now, before I call a screen done, I ask myself a few questions:

- What happens when this is empty?
- What happens when it's slow?
- What happens when someone's name is forty characters long?

Nobody applauds those states, but they're where people actually spend their time.

## The code is a conversation

It works the other way too. Sometimes I'm halfway through building something and realise the flow doesn't feel right. In a pure engineering role I might just ship it anyway. Because I own the design too, I can stop, go back to the file and fix the idea, not just the implementation.

That loop is my favourite part of the job. Redesigning the admin panel at IESL taught me this more than anything: the best decisions came from moving back and forth, not from finishing one phase and handing it over.

## What nobody tells you

Holding both roles is tiring in a quiet way. There's always one more pixel to adjust and one more edge case to handle, and you're the only person who'll notice either. I'm learning to decide what "good enough to ship" means before I start, not at midnight.[^2]

It's also lonely sometimes. When the designer and the developer are the same person, nobody pushes back. So I've started showing work early, to engineers, to friends, to anyone who'll look, because other eyes catch what mine have gotten used to.

## Why I keep doing it

When I'm working on Forge, I can have an idea over breakfast and hold it in my hand, running on my phone, by the evening. I don't think I'll ever get tired of that.

Design taught me to care about the person on the other side of the screen. Engineering taught me to respect what it takes to put something there. I'd like to keep getting better at both, and I'll keep writing about it here as I go.

[^1]: Real data is always messier than the mockup. Always.
